What's in a receipt

Four pieces, each one checks the others.

01 · BYTES

Exact bytes

The request and the response exactly as they were sent and received, page images included, with their SHA-256. Change one byte and the hashes no longer match.

02 · SIGNATURE

Signature

An ECDSA signature by the enclave over model:sha256(request):sha256(response). It recovers to the signing address, and that address must be the one in the attestation.

03 · ATTESTATION

Attestation

An Intel TDX quote whose report data binds the signing key to a fresh nonce, so the report was produced for this request and no other.

04 · TCB

TCB status

The quote is checked against Intel's collateral, fetched through the relay because Intel's service has no CORS. The CLI does the same.

From the terminal

Same checks, no browser.

Honest limits

What it does not check yet.

  • The verifier does not yet replay the RTMR3 event log against the quote, and does not check GPU evidence. The receipt keeps both, so they can be added without re-running anything.
  • A refused payment is stored small on chain: the contract keeps the first 64 characters of its attestation reference, so its reference matches on those 64 characters.
  • When the quote verifier or the relay cannot be reached, the browser shows the TCB check as skipped; the CLI checks it.

Bursar is software, not a payment service. Funds stay in the company's own contract, under the company's keys. USDC only. Sample invoices are fictional.
